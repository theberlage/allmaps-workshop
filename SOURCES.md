## Haags Gemeentearchief

- [Haags Gemeentearchief](https://haagsgemeentearchief.nl/)

Some of the images from the "Beeldcollectie" can be opened in Allmaps directly. If you make a new bookmark, paste the code below and click on it on a media record page, it will try to open the image in Allmaps Editor. If this doesn't work, you can download it and add it to the SURFdrive folder.

[Test the bookmarklet on this page](https://haagsgemeentearchief.nl/mediabank/beeldcollectie/detail/135c9ab3-34a6-1eff-0ccf-7b3a84053e74/media/63fd63ce-05b0-5567-854f-fa4162cbf9ea?mode=detail&view=horizontal&q=kadaster&rows=1&page=1)

```js
javascript: (async function () {
const apiKey = document.getElementsByTagName("pic-mediabank")[0].getAttribute("data-api-key");
const baseUrl = document.getElementsByTagName("pic-mediabank")[0].getAttribute("data-api-url");
const matches = location.href.match(/\/detail\/(.+?)\/media\/(.+?)\?/);
const mediaId = matches[1];
const imageId = matches[2];
const apiUrl = baseUrl + "media/" + mediaId + "?apiKey=" + apiKey;
const resp = await fetch(apiUrl).then(resp => resp.json());
const asset = resp.media[0].asset.filter(asset => asset.uuid === imageId)[0];
const allmapsEditorBase = "https://editor.allmaps.org/#/collection?url=";
const iiifUrl = asset.topview.replace("topviewjson/memorix","iiif");
location = allmapsEditorBase + iiifUrl;
})()
```

## Per theme

### The coast as a defense line

Cases:
- Hoek van Holland: strategic site along the Nieuwe Waterweg with a long history of military infrastructure that was transformed to become a key part of the Atlantikwall 
- Scheveningen: urban area partially evacuated and demolished for the construction of the military infrastructure, including postwar reconstruction of the city
- Both with adjacent “Freie Küste”

Sources Hoek van Holland and Scheveningen:
- [National Archives, 2.13.167 Inventaris van het archief van het 2e Geniecommandement, Bureau Registratie Verdedigingswerken van het Ministerie van Defensie (zgn. 'Bunkerarchief'), (1923) 1946-1987 (1992).](https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/2.13.167)

### The coast as a bathing site

Cases:
- Scheveningen: Upper class coastal leisure and healthcare, looked at through the development of bathhouses in Scheveningen, from the first wooden building opened in Scheveningen (1818–1820) replaced by a stone building (1820–1828), to the municipal bathhouse (1828–1884), to the Kurhaus (1885) and related pier and boulevard. 
- Hoek van Holland: Mass recreation, from the introduction of the first Dutch holiday park in Hoek van Holland (1933), following the 1924 campsite, and municipal exploitation of the coastal strip with beach pavilions and other recreational uses. 

Sources Hoek van Holland:
- Rotterdam City Archives, 406-01 Archieven van de Hulpsecretarie Hoek van Holland, Archief van de Hulpsecretarie Hoek van Holland (1907-1943), Grond (strandbeheer), 33 Stukken betreffende het verpachten van percelen strand voor de periode 1931-1939, 1930. Met tekeningen en foto's van strandtenten. Not available digitally, make photos at the archive
- Rotterdam City Archives, P64 Holiday homes Hoek van Holland, 1951: not available online, but digital files available 
- Rotterdam City Archives, Portefeuillle_B5C, maps of the campsite, 1937: not available online, but digital files available

Sources Scheveningen:

### The coast as a dune landscape

Cases:
- Mouth of the Nieuwe Waterweg, with the settlement of Hoek van Holland on one side—including the lighthouse keepers' houses as key to the safety system—and nature reserve De Beer—vanished in the 1960s with the expansion of the Rotterdam port—on the other.
- Meijendel, drinking water company for The Hague, with the pumping station and water tower Scheveningen (1874), gradually expanding and going deeper into the ground, until river water is ‘imported’ with infiltration system river water (1955)
- Monster, Ter Heijde and the Delfland coast. A fishing village that for centuries has followed the dynamics of the coastal landscape, moving further and further inland, until the consolidation of the coast. Demolition and reconstruction for sanitary purposes in the 1930s. Plans for a Boschplan, to ‘beautify’ the dunes and provide a space for leisure. Demolition during WWII. Strengthening of the coast up to the defined Delta height and profile (1955 Deltaplan and 1958 Deltawet), with left-over material from the construction of the port extension.
